The Teacher Multiplier

The Future of School
Begins With the Teacher.

Schools are expected to protect examination performance while preparing students for a world shaped by AI, information overload, changing careers, competency-based assessment and greater learner diversity.

The answer is not to abandon academic rigour. It is to help teachers make knowledge more coherent, transferable, assessable and responsive to the learner in front of them.

Inside the Lab

Teachers Learn by
Doing the Work.

The experience moves through demonstration, design, critique and rehearsal—not extended lecture blocks.

01

Brain Before Bell

Cognitive load, schemas, retrieval, spacing, dual coding and metacognition are experienced through live learning demonstrations.

Output: redesigned explanation + retrieval plan
02

Global Practice Safari

Teams investigate selected practices from multiple systems and defend what they would retain, reframe or reject.

Output: Global Practice Transfer Canvas
03

THINK Questioning Lab

Hinge questions, wait time, accountable talk and inclusive participation are converted into a usable questioning sequence.

Output: question ladder + hinge question
04

Assessment Studio

Teachers diagnose question papers, redesign items, calibrate rubrics and connect errors to remedial action.

Output: assessment blueprint + error-analysis plan
05

UDL & Adaptive Teaching

One lesson is redesigned for different learner profiles without lowering the learning intention or core standard.

Output: inclusive adaptation grid
06

Inquiry & Project Lab

A decorative project is transformed into a rigorous challenge with curriculum outcomes, evidence, product and accountability.

Output: authentic project brief
07

AI-SAFE & Media Literacy

Teachers use AI with purpose, verify outputs, examine bias and provenance, and protect subject accuracy and student data.

Output: verified AI-supported teaching artefact
08

Micro-Teaching Arena

Teachers teach, observe evidence, receive developmental feedback, revise one move and rehearse again where the format allows.

Output: rubric + next rehearsal target
Memorable Enough to Use

A Small Set of
Practical Frameworks.

The participant-facing frameworks are deliberately limited so that teachers can remember them, apply them and show evidence of use.

3R

Retain • Reframe • Reject

Adapt global practice responsibly rather than copying it.

CLEAR

Context • Learning Intention • Experience / Explanation • Assessment • Reflection

Design one coherent, subject-specific lesson.

DRAFT

Diagnose • Respond • Act • Feedback • Try Again

Make feedback produce a learner action and reattempt.

AI-SAFE

Aim • Inputs • Sources • Accuracy • Fairness • Ethics

Use AI with purpose, verification and safeguards.

Questions Schools Ask

What This FDP
Is — and Is Not.

Will this replace CBSE / Matriculation or academic rigour?

No. The programme is designed to translate selected practices into the school's existing curriculum and examination reality. Strong content knowledge and board preparation remain essential.

Is this mainly an AI workshop?

No. AI and media literacy are one part of a broader teaching-and-learning programme covering learning science, questioning, assessment, inclusion, pedagogy, micro-teaching and implementation.

Which format should a school choose?

The two-day workshop is suited to whole-faculty exposure and a common school-wide language. The three-day lab is better for a focused transformation cohort, HODs and schools that want deeper rehearsal and a 90-day pilot.

Will teachers be publicly scored or ranked?

No. Observation and micro-teaching evidence are developmental. Management reporting should use anonymised trends and common development areas rather than public teacher ranking.
